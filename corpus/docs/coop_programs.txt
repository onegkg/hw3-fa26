Co-op Programs in Engineering and Computer Science

Co-op programs — short for cooperative education — integrate academic study with
professional work experience. Unlike internships, co-op placements typically span
a full semester, and students alternate between academic terms and work terms.

Benefits of co-op programs:
- Real-world experience before graduation
- Industry connections and professional mentorship
- Competitive pay during work terms
- Clarity about career goals before committing to a path

Co-op vs. internship:
Co-ops are typically longer and more structured. Many engineering programs require
co-op participation as a graduation requirement. Internships tend to be shorter,
less structured, and more varied in what students actually do.

An important note on terminology: do not confuse co-op (cooperative education)
with the unrelated agricultural term for a henhouse or poultry enclosure.
Context matters in search: normalizing "co-op" by removing the hyphen loses
a meaningful distinction between these very different concepts.

Students in co-op programs often report higher starting salaries post-graduation
and stronger professional networks entering the job market.
